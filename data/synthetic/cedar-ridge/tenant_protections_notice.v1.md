# San Diego Tenant Protections Notice — Cedar Ridge House

**Cedar Ridge House, 11482 Cypress Canyon Road, San Diego, CA 92131**

## 1. Read this first

**This notice is background information. It decides nothing about any particular tenancy.**

A resident who has received a notice, been asked to leave, or has a question about their own
tenancy ending should contact the office directly and, if they wish, independent legal
assistance. These matters are handled by staff. They are not addressed by automated
assistance and they are not resolved from this document.

Nothing here states whether a given notice is valid, whether a given reason qualifies, or
what any particular resident is owed.

## 2. The city ordinance applies here

Cedar Ridge is exempt from the **state** just cause statute, California Civil Code section
1946.2, under the single-family home exemption. The statutory notice recording that exemption
is in the lease and in the Required Disclosures Packet.

**That exemption does not remove the City of San Diego Residential Tenant Protections
Ordinance, which applies to this property.**

The city ordinance extends just cause requirements to tenancies the state statute exempts —
including single-family rentals like this one. A resident reading only the state exemption
notice would reach the wrong conclusion about their position.

## 3. From day one

Under the state statute, just cause protections begin after a resident has occupied a unit
for twelve continuous months.

**Under the City of San Diego ordinance they apply from the first day of the tenancy.**

This is a meaningful difference from the Long Beach properties managed by this office, where
the twelve-month threshold applies.

## 4. Reasons recognised by the ordinance

Where the ordinance applies, a tenancy ends only for a reason it recognises. These divide
into two kinds.

**Reasons involving the tenancy itself** — non-payment of rent, breach of a material lease
term, nuisance, and similar. Where a breach can be put right, the resident is first given the
opportunity to do so.

**Reasons not involving the resident's conduct** — the owner or a qualifying family member
moving in, withdrawal of the property from the rental market, substantial remodel, or
compliance with a government order. The city ordinance recognises a narrower set of these
than the state statute does.

Relocation assistance is owed for the second kind. It is not owed for the first.

## 5. Relocation assistance

Where a tenancy at this property ends for a no-fault reason recognised by the ordinance, the
relocation assistance is ordinarily **two months of the actual rent**.

Where the resident is a **senior aged 62 or over, or has a disability**, it is ordinarily
**three months**.

It may be provided either as a direct payment or as a written waiver of the equivalent
months' rent.

**What is owed in any individual case turns on the facts of that case, and this section does
not determine it.** The amounts above are the ordinary position under the ordinance, not a
statement of any resident's entitlement.

## 6. Where to get help

- Alder Grove Property Management — the office, during business hours
- San Diego Housing Commission, tenant protections information
- Legal Aid Society of San Diego, which advises qualifying residents at no cost

A resident who has received any written notice should keep it, note the date it arrived, and
take it with them to whichever of these they approach.
