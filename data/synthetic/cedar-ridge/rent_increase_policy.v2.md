# Rent Increase & Renewal Policy — Cedar Ridge House

**Cedar Ridge House, 11482 Cypress Canyon Road, San Diego, CA 92131**
Effective 1 April 2025.

## 1. No statutory rent cap applies

**There is no percentage cap on rent increases at this property.**

Cedar Ridge is exempt from the rent limits in California Civil Code section 1947.12. The
annual percentage limits that apply at the Long Beach properties managed by this office —
8.7% for the cap year beginning 1 August 2026 — **do not apply here.**

This is the point residents are most often surprised by, and it is the reason the exemption
notice appears in the lease.

The absence of a statutory cap does not mean rent rises, or rises by any particular amount.
It means the limit is not fixed by statute.

## 2. Why the exemption applies

Section 1947.12 does not apply to a single-family home where both of the following are true:

1. the owner is not a real estate investment trust, a corporation, or a limited liability
   company with at least one corporate member; and
2. the required statutory notice was given to the resident in writing.

Cedar Ridge is a detached single-family home and both conditions are satisfied. The
statutory notice appears in full in the Required Disclosures Packet, section 2, in the AB
1482 Exemption Addendum, and in the signed lease.

**The notice must be in the lease at signing.** It cannot be added to a tenancy afterwards,
and a tenancy where it was omitted is not exempt.

## 3. What the exemption does not affect

The exemption concerns the **state rent cap** and the **state just cause statute**. It does
not touch the following, all of which apply at this property:

- **City of San Diego just cause protections**, which apply **from the first day of the
  tenancy**
- notice requirements for a rent increase, in section 4 below
- notice requirements for ending a tenancy
- the security deposit limit, the 21-day accounting, and every other deposit rule
- the entry notice requirement under Civil Code section 1954
- prohibitions on retaliatory and discriminatory conduct

A resident should not read "exempt from the rent cap" as "no protections apply." See the
Required Disclosures Packet, section 3.

## 4. Notice of a rent increase

A rent increase takes effect only after written notice.

- An increase of **10% or less** within any twelve-month period requires **30 days'** written
  notice.
- An increase of **more than 10%** within any twelve-month period requires **90 days'**
  written notice.

Because no percentage cap applies at this property, **an increase above 10% is possible
here** in a way it is not at the Long Beach properties — and where that happens the 90-day
notice applies.

The notice states the new amount and the date from which it applies, and is posted to the
resident portal.

## 5. Frequency

Rent is not increased during a fixed term unless the signed lease expressly provides for it.

On a month-to-month tenancy an increase requires notice under section 4.

## 6. Renewal

The office contacts the resident about renewal ordinarily 60 to 90 days before a fixed term
ends.

Where no new fixed term is signed, the tenancy continues month to month on the existing
terms, subject to any change made by notice under section 4.

## 7. The resident's own rent

This document states the rules that apply at this property. **It does not state the rent,
whether the rent is being increased, by how much, or from when.**

A resident asking about an increase to their own rent is directed to the notice itself, to
the resident portal, or to the office.
