## 1. What this addendum is

California Civil Code section 1954.603 requires that residents be given written information
about bed bugs before signing a lease. This addendum provides it and is attached to every
lease at Cedar Ridge House.

## 2. About bed bugs

Bed bugs are small, flat, wingless insects, reddish-brown, about the size of an apple seed
when adult. They feed on blood, usually at night.

They are not a sign of poor housekeeping and are not known to transmit disease. They arrive
on luggage, clothing, second-hand furniture, and belongings.

Reactions to bites vary widely. Some people develop itchy welts, others nothing at all. Bites
alone do not establish an infestation.

A detached house has no shared walls with another tenancy, so an infestation here does not
spread between units the way it can in an apartment building. It can still spread between
rooms, and it will not resolve on its own.

## 3. Signs of an infestation

- live insects in mattress seams, box spring, bed frame joints, headboard, or furniture seams
- small dark spots on bedding, mattress, or nearby surfaces — digested blood
- shed skins, pale and translucent
- small white eggs lodged in crevices
- rusty or reddish staining on sheets

Bed bugs shelter within a few feet of where people sleep, in cracks and seams rather than in
the open.

## 4. Reporting

**Report suspected bed bugs to the office promptly.** Early reporting makes treatment faster,
easier, and less disruptive, and limits how far through the house the problem travels.

## 5. What residents should not do

- **Do not attempt your own treatment.** Over-the-counter sprays and foggers typically scatter
  an infestation rather than eliminate it, driving insects into wall voids and adjoining
  rooms.
- Do not move mattresses, bedding, or upholstered furniture into the garage. The garage
  becomes a reservoir and the problem returns.
- Do not discard infested furniture at the kerb without telling the office; it requires
  wrapping and marking, and bulky item collection has to be arranged.
- Do not delay reporting out of embarrassment. Bed bugs are not a reflection on the resident.

## 6. Notification of inspection results

Where the office inspects the property, the resident is informed of the results **within two
business days** of the inspection.

## 7. Preparing for treatment

Treatment normally requires the resident to prepare the property — laundering bedding and
clothing on a hot cycle, bagging items, moving furniture clear of walls, and providing
access. The office issues specific instructions in advance.

More than one visit is usually needed. The resident is asked to allow access for follow-up
visits, which confirm the treatment succeeded.

Where treatment covers the whole house, the resident may need to be out of the property for a
period. The office gives notice of that in advance.

## 8. What the owner does not do

The owner does not show, rent, or re-rent the property while it is known to be currently
infested.

The owner does not treat a report of bites alone as establishing an infestation; physical
evidence is needed.
