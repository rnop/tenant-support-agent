## 1. What this addendum is

California Civil Code sections 1947.12 and 1946.2 exempt certain single-family homes from the
statewide rent cap and the statewide just cause requirement — but only where the owner gives
the resident a written notice in the exact wording the statute prescribes.

This addendum is that notice. It is attached to every lease at Cedar Ridge House.

## 2. The statutory notice

The following is reproduced in the exact statutory wording. It is not paraphrased, and it is
not to be paraphrased, because a paraphrase does not satisfy the statute.

> This property is not subject to the rent limits imposed by Section 1947.12 of the Civil
> Code and is not subject to the just cause requirements of Section 1946.2 of the Civil
> Code. This property meets the requirements of Sections 1947.12(d)(5) and 1946.2(e)(8) of
> the Civil Code and the owner is not any of the following: (1) a real estate investment
> trust, as defined by Section 856 of the Internal Revenue Code; (2) a corporation; or (3) a
> limited liability company in which at least one member is a corporation.

## 3. Why this property qualifies

The exemption is available where **both** of the following are true:

1. **The property is a single-family home or condominium.** Cedar Ridge is a detached
   single-family home let as a whole.
2. **The owner is not a real estate investment trust, a corporation, or a limited liability
   company with at least one corporate member.** The owner of this property is none of these.

Both conditions are satisfied here.

Note that the second condition is about the **type** of owner, not the number of properties
an owner holds. It is a different test from the small-landlord exception in the security
deposit rules, which turns on property and unit counts. **The two tests are unrelated and one
does not imply the other.** See the Security Deposit Policy for the deposit position at this
property, which is one month's rent.

## 4. The notice must be given at signing

**This notice must be contained in the lease at the time the lease is signed, or in an
addendum executed at the same time.**

It cannot be added to a tenancy afterwards. Where the notice was not given at signing, the
property is **not** exempt and the statutory rent cap and state just cause requirement apply
to that tenancy.

This is why the notice appears in three places in the Cedar Ridge document set — this
addendum, the Required Disclosures Packet, and the lease itself.

## 5. What the exemption does not do

The exemption is narrower than it looks. It concerns the **state** rent cap and the **state**
just cause statute, and nothing else.

**It does not remove the City of San Diego Residential Tenant Protections Ordinance**, which
applies to this property and which extends just cause requirements to tenancies that section
1946.2 exempts. Under the city ordinance those protections apply **from the first day of the
tenancy**, not after twelve months.

Also unaffected, and applying in full at this property:

- notice periods for ending a tenancy under Civil Code section 1946.1
- notice periods for a rent increase under Civil Code section 827
- the one month security deposit limit and the 21-day deposit accounting
- the 24-hour entry notice requirement under Civil Code section 1954
- habitability obligations
- prohibitions on retaliatory and discriminatory conduct
- fair housing law, including in relation to assistance animals

**A resident should not read "exempt from AB 1482" as "no protections apply."**

**Questions about termination of a tenancy, about a notice the resident has received, or
about relocation assistance are handled by staff.**
