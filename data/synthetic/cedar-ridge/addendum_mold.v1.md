## 1. What this addendum is

Indoor mold follows moisture. Almost every indoor mold problem begins with a water source —
a leak, condensation, or humidity that is not ventilated away. Control the moisture and mold
does not establish.

This addendum sets out what each party does about moisture at this property.

## 2. The owner's statement

The owner is **not aware of any mold at this property** exceeding levels known to affect
health, other than mold visible on ordinary inspection.

The property was inspected before the tenancy and any moisture damage found was repaired.

## 3. Where moisture enters a detached house

A detached house has moisture paths an apartment does not, and the resident is the only
person on site to notice them:

- **roof and gutters** — blocked gutters overflow against the wall and into the eaves
- **irrigation** — sprinkler heads directed at the house wet the stucco and the foundation
- **grading** — soil or mulch banked against the wall holds water at the base
- **crawl space and under-floor areas**, where these exist
- **the water heater and its pan**, in the garage
- **washing machine hoses**, which fail without warning
- **exterior doors and window seals**, particularly on the weather side

## 4. What the resident agrees to do

- Run the bathroom fan during and for at least 20 minutes after showering or bathing.
- Run the kitchen fan when cooking, especially when boiling or steaming.
- Ventilate the house. Open windows when weather allows.
- Wipe down surfaces where condensation collects.
- Leave a gap between furniture and exterior walls.
- **Keep ground-accessible gutters and downpipes clear of leaf litter.**
- **Check that irrigation heads are not spraying the house**, and report any that are.
- Keep soil, mulch, and stored items away from the base of exterior walls.
- Dry spills and overflows promptly.
- Keep the area around the water heater clear and dry.
- Not block or disable any ventilation fan or vent.

## 5. What the resident agrees to report

Promptly, to the office:

- any leak, drip, or water intrusion, however small
- any overflow from a bath, shower, sink, toilet, or washing machine
- **water pooling against an exterior wall, or an irrigation head spraying the house**
- **a gutter overflowing during rain**
- any damp patch, staining, bubbling paint, or swollen woodwork
- any visible mold growth
- a persistent musty or earthy smell
- a ventilation fan that does not work
- a window or door admitting rain
- any sign of moisture in the garage or around the water heater

**Water intrusion is urgent maintenance.** Because nobody else is on site, the resident's
report is usually the first anyone knows of it.

## 6. What the owner does

On receiving a report the owner inspects, identifies and repairs the moisture source, and
remediates affected material.

Remediation beyond a small surface area is carried out by contractors experienced in mold
remediation.

The owner is responsible for roof, gutter, grading, and irrigation repairs, and for tree work
above ground-accessible height.

## 7. Access

Inspection and remediation require access, including to the yard. Other than in an emergency
the office gives at least 24 hours' written notice. Substantial water intrusion is an
emergency and does not require advance notice.
