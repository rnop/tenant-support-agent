# Maintenance & Repair Policy — Cedar Ridge House

**Cedar Ridge House, 11482 Cypress Canyon Road, San Diego, CA 92131**

## 1. How to make a request

Non-emergency requests go through the **resident portal**, which timestamps the request and
keeps a record. Requests may also be made by telephone or in writing to the office.

Describe the problem, where it is, and when it started. Requests are acknowledged and the
resident is told the priority assigned.

Because this is a detached house rather than a managed building, a contractor visit usually
requires an appointment arranged with the resident rather than a general access window.

## 2. Priority levels

**Emergency.** Any condition in section 3. Attended immediately, at any hour.

**Urgent.** A condition materially affecting habitability or security without being an
immediate danger — a failed water heater, a failed refrigerator, no air conditioning in
extreme heat, the only functioning toilet out of service, a contained but persistent leak, a
failed exterior door lock, an inoperable garage door leaving the house unsecured. Target
first response within **24 hours**.

**Routine.** Everything else — dripping taps, running toilets, faulty light fittings and
switches, sticking windows and doors, worn seals, cosmetic damage, minor irrigation faults.
Target first response within **five business days**.

Target times are for a first response, which may be an inspection rather than a completed
repair.

## 3. What counts as an emergency

Treated as emergencies at this property:

- gas odour or a suspected gas leak
- smoke or fire
- flooding, or an uncontrolled water leak
- no heat
- carbon monoxide detector activation, or suspected carbon monoxide
- no water, or total loss of water service
- an electrical hazard — sparking, burning smell, exposed live wiring, failed panel
- a broken exterior door lock, broken exterior window, or any condition leaving the house
  unsecured
- a sewage backup into the house
- a fallen tree or limb affecting the structure or access
- any condition making the house unsafe to occupy

**Emergencies are reported by telephone, not through the portal.**

## 4. Reporting an emergency

Emergencies are reported to the office number. **Where anyone is in immediate danger, contact
emergency services first and the office afterwards.**

The office exists to take the report and dispatch. What a resident should do in the moment
depends on the situation and is directed by emergency services or by the person taking the
call.

## 5. After-hours answering service

Outside office hours the office number is answered by an **answering service**, not by staff.

The service takes the report, pages the on-call manager, and confirms a callback. Expect a
callback rather than an immediate resolution on the call itself.

This differs from the Long Beach properties, which have a directly staffed 24-hour
maintenance line. The service here is an answering service and it does not dispatch
contractors on its own authority.

Because the callback is part of the process, the resident should give a number that will be
answered.

## 6. Access to the property

Repairs need access. Other than in an emergency the office gives at least **24 hours' written
notice**, stating date, approximate time, and purpose, and attends during normal business
hours.

**No advance notice is required in an emergency.**

Entry into the **yard** — for irrigation repair, tree work, or landscaping inspection — is
subject to the same 24-hour notice.

Where a pet is kept the office asks that it be secured, and that any gate the contractor
needs is unlocked.

## 7. Owner responsibilities

The owner is responsible for:

- the roof, exterior walls, and structure
- plumbing, gas, and electrical systems
- the water heater
- the heating and air conditioning system, including annual servicing
- the built-in appliances supplied with the house
- the garage door mechanism
- exterior painting
- **irrigation system repairs**
- **tree work above ground-accessible height**
- termite and structural pest treatment
- working smoke and carbon monoxide detectors at the start of the tenancy

## 8. Resident responsibilities

The resident is responsible for:

- keeping the house clean and sanitary
- **replacing HVAC filters at least quarterly** — a clogged filter that damages the system is
  a resident-caused repair
- **replacing smoke and carbon monoxide detector batteries**, and reporting any detector that
  does not function
- replacing light bulbs, interior and exterior
- resetting tripped breakers and the garbage disposal before reporting a fault
- clearing drain blockages the resident introduced
- keeping ground-accessible gutters clear of leaf litter
- **placing refuse and recycling bins at the kerb on collection day and returning them the
  same day**
- reporting leaks, damp, visible mold, and pest activity promptly

## 9. Yard and irrigation

The resident maintains the yard — mowing, edging, watering, trimming within reach from the
ground, and weeding. See the Standard Residential Lease Terms, section 11.

The resident operates the irrigation system on a reasonable schedule and **reports faults**
rather than attempting repairs. A broken head, a leaking valve, or a controller failure is
reported to the office and repaired at the owner's cost.

Planting lost because the irrigation was not run is a resident-caused loss. Planting lost to
a fault that was reported promptly is not.

Where water restrictions are in force, the resident follows them; planting lost to lawful
restriction is not charged to the resident.

## 10. Damage beyond ordinary wear

Repairs needed because of damage caused by the resident, the household, or a guest, beyond
ordinary wear, are charged to the resident.

Ordinary wear is not charged. Carpet worn in traffic routes, faded paint, and small nail
holes are ordinary wear.

Where a charge applies the resident is told before work proceeds, other than in an emergency
that cannot wait.

## 11. Status of a specific request

This document explains how maintenance works here. **It holds no information about the status
of any request, the schedule for any visit, or the history of this property.**

A resident asking about a request they have made is directed to the resident portal, which
shows open requests, or to the office.
