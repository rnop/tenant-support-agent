## 1. What this addendum is

California Civil Code section 1940.9 requires the owner to disclose how utility charges are
determined before a lease is signed. This addendum does that for Harborview.

**The arrangement here is submetering, not allocation.** Maple Court across the street uses
allocation, and the two work differently. Residents should not assume they match.

## 2. Water is submetered

Harborview was built in **2004** and **each of the four units has its own water submeter**.

Consumption is measured per unit. Nothing about water billing here depends on occupancy,
square footage, or a fixed share.

## 3. How water is billed

Each unit is billed for **the water it actually uses**, as recorded by its own submeter.

Submeters are read monthly. The resident statement shows:

- the opening reading for the period
- the closing reading for the period
- the volume consumed
- the rate applied
- the resulting charge

The rate applied is the rate the property is charged by the provider for the same period.

Water serving the shared areas — the patio tap and the landscape irrigation — is on the
property's own meter and is **not** passed to residents.

## 4. Sewer and trash

Sewer and trash are not metered per unit. They are billed as a **flat monthly charge per
unit**, identical for all four units, and shown as a separate line.

## 5. No markup

The owner adds **no administrative charge, service fee, or margin** to any utility charge.
Amounts billed to residents reflect the provider's charges.

## 6. Billing cycle

Utility charges are billed **monthly, one month in arrears**, on the resident statement in
the portal.

They are not rent. A dispute about a utility charge does not excuse payment of rent, and an
unpaid utility charge is not treated as unpaid rent.

## 7. Right to readings and bills

**The resident may request their own submeter readings and a copy of the provider's bill for
any period in which they were charged.** Requests go to the office and are provided at no
cost, ordinarily within five business days.

Readings for the current and prior periods are also visible in the resident portal.

## 8. Disputes

A resident who believes a charge is wrong should write to the office identifying the period
and the reason. The office re-reads the submeter, checks it against the provider bill, and
replies in writing.

Where an error is found, the correction appears on the next statement.

## 9. Submeter faults

A submeter that fails or reads implausibly is repaired or replaced by the owner at the
owner's cost.

For any period in which a submeter was not recording correctly, the unit is billed at **the
average of its own consumption over the preceding three complete periods**, not at an
estimate based on other units.

Residents should report a suspected submeter fault to the office. Residents must not tamper
with, obstruct, or attempt to read or reset a submeter themselves.

## 10. Utilities in the resident's own name

The resident opens electricity and gas accounts effective on the lease start date, keeps
them current, and closes them at the end of the tenancy.

Allowing these to lapse can damage the unit, and the resident is responsible for damage that
results.
