## 1. What this addendum is

California Civil Code section 1954.603 requires that residents be given written information
about bed bugs before signing a lease. This addendum provides it and is attached to every
lease at Harborview.

## 2. About bed bugs

Bed bugs are small, flat, wingless insects, reddish-brown, about the size of an apple seed
when adult. They feed on blood, usually at night.

They are not a sign of poor housekeeping and are not known to transmit disease. They arrive
on luggage, clothing, second-hand furniture, and belongings.

Reactions to bites vary widely. Some people develop itchy welts, others nothing at all.
Bites on their own do not establish an infestation.

## 3. Signs of an infestation

- live insects in mattress seams, box spring, bed frame joints, headboard, or furniture
  seams
- small dark spots on bedding, mattress, or nearby surfaces — digested blood
- shed skins, pale and translucent
- small white eggs lodged in crevices
- rusty or reddish staining on sheets

Bed bugs shelter within a few feet of where people sleep, in cracks and seams rather than in
the open.

## 4. Reporting

**Report suspected bed bugs to the office promptly.** Early reporting makes treatment
faster, easier, and less disruptive, and reduces the chance of spread.

## 5. What residents should not do

- **Do not attempt your own treatment.** Over-the-counter sprays and foggers typically
  scatter an infestation rather than eliminate it, driving insects into wall voids and
  adjoining units. In a four-unit building that means the problem reaches neighbours.
- Do not move mattresses, bedding, or upholstered furniture to another unit, to the bike
  room, to the laundry room, or to the patio.
- Do not discard infested furniture at the trash enclosure without telling the office; it is
  a route for spread and requires wrapping and marking.
- Do not delay reporting out of embarrassment. Bed bugs are not a reflection on the
  resident.

## 6. Notification of inspection results

Where the office inspects a unit, the resident of that unit is informed of the results
**within two business days** of the inspection.

Where a shared area is inspected, **every resident** is informed of the results within two
business days.

## 7. Preparing for treatment

Treatment normally requires the resident to prepare the unit — laundering bedding and
clothing on a hot cycle, bagging items, moving furniture clear of walls, and providing
access. The office issues specific instructions in advance of any treatment.

More than one visit is usually needed. Residents are asked to allow access for follow-up
visits, which confirm the treatment succeeded.

## 8. What the owner does not do

The owner does not show, rent, or re-rent a unit known to be currently infested.

The owner does not treat a report of bites alone as establishing an infestation; physical
evidence is needed.
