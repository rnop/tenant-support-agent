# Maintenance & Repair Policy — Maple Court

**Maple Court, 3140 Roswell Avenue, Long Beach, CA 90814**

## 1. How to make a request

Non-emergency maintenance requests are submitted through the **resident portal**, which is
the preferred channel because it timestamps the request and keeps a record of it.

Requests may also be made by telephone or in person at the office during business hours,
or in writing.

A request should describe the problem, the room or location, and when it started. Requests
are acknowledged, and the resident is told the priority assigned.

## 2. Priority levels

**Emergency.** A condition described in section 3. Responded to immediately, at any hour.

**Urgent.** A condition that materially affects the habitability or security of the unit but
is not an immediate danger — for example a non-functioning refrigerator, a single
non-functioning toilet in a one-bathroom unit, a persistent leak that is being contained, a
failed door lock on an interior door, or deteriorating paint. Target response within **24
hours**.

**Routine.** Everything else — for example a dripping tap, a running toilet, a faulty light
fixture, a sticking window, a worn appliance seal, or cosmetic damage. Target response
within **five business days**.

Target response times are the target for a first response, which may be an inspection
rather than a completed repair. Parts availability and contractor scheduling can extend the
time to completion.

## 3. What counts as an emergency

The following are treated as emergencies at this property:

- gas odour or a suspected gas leak
- smoke or fire
- flooding, or an uncontrolled water leak
- no heat
- a suspected carbon monoxide alarm or carbon monoxide detector activation
- no water, or a total loss of water service to the unit
- an electrical hazard — sparking, burning smell, exposed live wiring, or a failed panel
- a broken exterior door lock, a broken exterior window, or any condition leaving the unit
  unsecured
- a sewage backup into the unit
- a condition that makes the unit unsafe to occupy

**An emergency is reported by telephone, not through the portal.** See section 4.

## 4. Reporting an emergency

Emergencies are reported to the **24-hour maintenance line**, which is answered at all
hours including weekends and holidays. The number is on the Property Fact Sheet, in the
portal, and posted in the laundry room.

Where there is an immediate danger to people, residents should contact emergency services
first and the maintenance line afterwards.

The maintenance line is for reporting. The instructions a resident should follow in an
emergency depend on the emergency and are given by emergency services or by the person
answering the line.

## 5. Access to the unit

Repairs require access. Except in an emergency, the office gives at least **24 hours'
written notice** before entering, stating the date, the approximate time, and the purpose,
and enters during normal business hours.

**No advance notice is required in an emergency.**

A resident may agree to a shorter notice period or to a specific appointment time. A
resident who prefers to be present should say so when making the request.

Where a resident has a pet, the office asks that the animal be secured during the visit.

## 6. Owner responsibilities

The owner maintains the unit in a habitable condition, including:

- effective waterproofing of roof and exterior walls
- plumbing, gas, and electrical systems in good working order
- hot and cold running water and a functioning sewage connection
- heating in working order
- floors, stairs, and railings in good repair
- working smoke and carbon monoxide detectors at the start of the tenancy
- the building, grounds, and shared areas kept clean and free of pests

The owner maintains the courtyard, the landscaping, the exterior of the building, and the
laundry room equipment.

## 7. Resident responsibilities

Residents are responsible for:

- keeping the unit clean and sanitary
- disposing of rubbish properly, using the trash enclosure
- using plumbing, electrical, and gas fixtures properly
- **replacing smoke detector and carbon monoxide detector batteries during the tenancy**,
  and reporting any detector that does not function
- replacing light bulbs in unit fixtures
- reporting leaks, damp, visible mold, deteriorating paint, and pest activity promptly
- not disturbing floor tile, ceiling texture, or pipe insulation, which may contain
  asbestos in a building of this age

## 8. Damage beyond ordinary wear

Repairs required because of damage caused by the resident, a member of the household, or a
guest, beyond ordinary wear and tear, are charged to the resident.

Ordinary wear is not charged. Worn carpet in traffic paths, faded paint, and minor nail
holes are ordinary wear.

Where a charge applies the resident is told before the work proceeds, other than in an
emergency where the work cannot wait.

## 9. Status of a specific request

This document describes how maintenance works at this property. **It does not contain the
status of any request, the schedule for any visit, or the history of any unit.**

A resident asking about a request they have made is directed to the resident portal, which
shows the status of open requests, or to the office.
