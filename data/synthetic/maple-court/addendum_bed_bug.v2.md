## 1. What this addendum is

California Civil Code section 1954.603 requires that residents receive written information
about bed bugs before a lease is signed. This addendum provides it and is attached to every
lease at Maple Court.

## 2. General information about bed bugs

Bed bugs are small, flat, wingless insects, reddish-brown, roughly the size of an apple
seed when fully grown. They feed on the blood of humans and animals, usually at night.

Bed bugs are not a sign of poor housekeeping and they are not known to transmit disease.
They travel on luggage, clothing, second-hand furniture, and personal belongings, which is
how they enter a building.

Reactions to bites vary. Some people develop itchy welts; others show no reaction at all.
Bites alone do not confirm an infestation.

## 3. How to recognise an infestation

Look for:

- live insects in mattress seams, box spring, bed frame joints, headboard, and furniture
  seams
- small dark spots on bedding, mattress, or nearby surfaces — digested blood
- shed skins, pale and translucent
- small white eggs in crevices
- rusty or reddish stains on sheets

Bed bugs shelter within a few feet of where people sleep, in cracks and seams rather than
in the open.

## 4. Reporting

**Residents are asked to report suspected bed bugs to the office promptly.** Early
reporting makes treatment substantially easier, faster, and less disruptive.

Residents should not attempt their own treatment. Over-the-counter sprays and foggers
commonly scatter an infestation into adjoining rooms and units, which in a four-unit
building means the problem reaches neighbours.

Residents should not move furniture or bedding to another unit or to a shared area.

## 5. Notification of inspection results

Where the office inspects a unit, the resident of that unit is informed of the results
**within two business days** of the inspection.

Where a shared area is inspected, **all residents** are informed of the results within two
business days.

## 6. Preparing for treatment

Treatment normally requires the resident to prepare the unit — laundering bedding and
clothing on a hot cycle, bagging items, moving furniture away from walls, and allowing
access. The office provides specific instructions before any treatment.

Treatment usually requires more than one visit. Residents are asked to allow access for
follow-up visits, which confirm the treatment worked.

## 7. What the owner does not do

The owner does not show, rent, or re-rent a unit that is known to be currently infested.

The owner does not conclude from a report of bites alone that a unit is infested;
confirmation requires physical evidence.
